* Preamble — why a doctrine at all

A discipline-layer can be either /a set of habits/ or /a written doctrine/. The
former lives in the heads of people who learned it from other people; it
travels by apprenticeship. The latter lives on disk; it travels by reading.

When a research group is small, when its members all came up through the same
lab, and when the data passes between hands that already know what the data
means, habits are enough. The register of a column is /understood/. The mode
of a figure is /felt/. The discipline is in the air of the room.

That arrangement has become brittle. Two things broke it:

1. *Open data became the norm.* A dataset published to EnviDat or to a
   thematic repository is consumed downstream by people who never met its
   producer. The habits that held the register stable in the producing lab
   do not travel with the CSV.

2. *Large language models entered the workflow.* The scientist who once
   wrote their own quality-control script now asks an assistant to "tidy
   this up". The assistant is helpful, and helpfulness — in the absence of
   register-awareness — silently demotes measurements into derivations,
   silently smooths sensor artefacts that were the actual scientific signal,
   silently fills gaps with plausible values that pass downstream
   sanity-checks but encode a generative model the manifest does not declare.

Neither of these is fraud. Neither is even error in the conventional sense.
Both are the natural failure mode of a /workflow whose register-discipline
lives in the heads of people who are no longer at the keyboard/.

~sakshi~ is the discipline made external. It is a small written doctrine, a
small schema, and a small enforcement tool. The doctrine is the
load-bearing part. The schema and the enforcement tool are implementations
of the doctrine. You can re-implement them. You should not re-invent the
doctrine without good reason.

* The four registers

Every data artefact in a scientific-data pipeline — every column in a
DataFrame, every file on disk, every value in a model output, every pixel
in a figure — has an /epistemic status/. It came from somewhere. The
somewhere matters.

We name four registers. They are not exhaustive — the fourth is a
deliberate catch-all for everything that is not the first three — but they
are sufficient to draw the firewall that matters.

** Register 1 — ~measurement~

/Definition/: a direct reading from a sensor, an observation, or a recorded
event in the physical world.

/Examples/: a temperature value read from a thermistor at IMIS station
WFJ (Weissfluhjoch) at 14:00 UTC on 2024-02-15. The number of cells of
a given type counted in a tissue slice. The pixel intensity at coordinate
~(i, j)~ in a Sentinel-2 tile, as delivered by the Copernicus catalogue
before any radiometric processing the consumer applies.

/Note/: ~measurement~ is the registration of *what was the case*. It is the
ground truth that the rest of the pipeline owes its existence to. It is
also the register that most needs protecting: the value of every
downstream artefact depends on whether the original measurement remains
recoverable.

/What it is not/: a "measurement" is not a /good/ measurement, a /calibrated/
measurement, a /quality-controlled/ measurement, or a /useful/ measurement.
A spurious sensor reading caused by a bird sitting on the radiation shield
is still a measurement. The register tag is about /origin/, not /quality/.

** Register 2 — ~derived-from-measurement~

/Definition/: a deterministic, documented function of one or more
measurements (or of other ~derived-from-measurement~ values), where the
derivation is recorded in the manifest and the inputs remain identifiable.

/Examples/: a daily mean temperature computed from hourly readings. A
gap-filled column where the gap-fill rule is /linear temporal interpolation
between neighbouring measurements within 6 hours/ — the rule is
deterministic, the inputs are measurements, the procedure is named. The
biomass density computed from cell counts and a known per-cell mass.

/Key property — recoverability/: given the manifest's record of the
derivation, the ~measurement~-register inputs, and the procedure, the
~derived-from-measurement~ value can be regenerated. If you cannot
regenerate it, it is not in this register; it is something else.

/Failure mode it catches/: silent in-place computation. A scientist
applies a smoothing filter to a "noisy" temperature column and overwrites
it. The column is now ~derived-from-measurement~, but its register tag was
never updated, the smoothing procedure was not recorded, and the original
measurements are no longer in the file. Downstream consumers will read
the column as if it were ~measurement~ and produce uncertainty estimates
that are too tight.

** Register 3 — ~synthetic-acknowledged~

/Definition/: a value generated by a model, a simulator, an LLM, a
sampling procedure, or any process whose output is *not* a deterministic
function of measurements — and whose synthetic status is declared in the
manifest.

/Examples/: gap-filled values produced by a learned imputation model.
Counterfactual scenarios from a numerical climate simulation. Predictions
from a trained statistical model. Strings produced by an LLM (whether
prose, code, or numerical estimates). Bootstrap-resampled values used to
estimate uncertainty.

/The "acknowledged" is load-bearing/. There is no register called
"synthetic-unacknowledged" in sakshi's vocabulary. A synthetic value that
is not declared as such is not a different register — it is a /failure/ of
the discipline. The compound name forces the question at write-time: /am
I willing to put this in writing?/

/Note on the LLM case/. Code generated by an LLM, when that code is
reviewed and committed by a human, is in a strange position. The code
itself is ~synthetic-acknowledged~ in its origin but, once accepted by
review, behaves as code: it is run, it produces outputs, those outputs
have their own register depending on what the code does. The doctrine's
position is that the *origin* is recorded in the manifest's
~llm_assistance:~ field (which model, what level of assistance, who
reviewed), and the *output* register is determined by what the code does
to its inputs, not by whether the code itself was LLM-written. This is
consistent with how we treat human-written code: the author is recorded,
the output register depends on the operation.

** Register 4 — ~expression-mode~

/Definition/: a rendering whose intent is to /communicate an experience or
an idea/, and whose claim to truth is *different in kind* from the claims
of registers 1–3.

/Examples/: an artistic visualisation that exaggerates a topographical
feature for emphasis. A pedagogical animation that uses fictional but
plausible data to illustrate a concept. A schematic figure where colours
are chosen for clarity rather than fidelity. A model rendered with
non-physical lighting for legibility. A text description of a phenomenon
written for a general audience.

/Key property — the firewall asymmetry/: an ~expression-mode~ artefact may
have ~measurement~ or ~derived-from-measurement~ inputs (a stylised plot
made from real data; a schematic informed by real geometry). But the
*output* is in expression-mode, and the output may *not* flow downstream
into a ~reconstruction-and-simulation~ pipeline step without an explicit
~re-measurement:~ declaration. You cannot read pixels off a stylised
figure and treat them as measurements. You cannot take a pedagogical
animation's coordinates as ground truth. The firewall is directional.

/Note/: ~expression-mode~ is not "low-quality" or "non-scientific". A
pedagogical animation can be more truthful than a literal plot of raw
data — it can convey what /matters/ where the literal plot conveys only
what was /sampled/. The point of the register is to mark which kind of
truth-claim is being made, so that downstream consumers know which
operations are permitted.

** Compound registers

A single artefact may carry more than one register tag, and often must.

/Example 1/. A gap-filled IMIS temperature column. Most values are
~derived-from-measurement~ (linear temporal interpolation). Some values
are ~synthetic-acknowledged~ (a learned model used where the gap was too
wide for interpolation). The column-level register is
~derived-from-measurement | synthetic-acknowledged~. A per-row register
column (often called a flag column in environmental-data conventions) is
required to distinguish.

/Example 2/. An ecological-suitability raster computed from a digital
elevation model (~measurement~ via SwissTopo), a vegetation classification
(~derived-from-measurement~ via a deterministic threshold on Sentinel-2
NDVI), and a climatic suitability layer from a learned bioclim model
(~synthetic-acknowledged~). The output raster is
~derived-from-measurement | synthetic-acknowledged~. The acknowledgement
in the compound forces downstream consumers to recognise that the raster
has a generative component, even if most of the pipeline upstream was
deterministic.

/The discipline/: compound registers are *required where applicable*. The
firewall refuses to collapse a compound to its dominant single register
for convenience. The reason is that the failure modes the firewall is
designed to catch — silent demotion, silent promotion — are usually
failures of the /minority/ register being lost, not the majority.

** The firewall

We can now state the firewall directly. It is short.

#+begin_quote
*No pipeline step may silently demote or promote a register.*

A *demotion* is an output whose register-tag is closer to ~measurement~
than its inputs and operation justify.

A *promotion* is an output whose register-tag is farther from
~measurement~ than its inputs and operation require, *unless* the
operation is declared.

The firewall holds when every register change between inputs and outputs
is named in the manifest's ~derivation:~ or ~synthesis:~ field, and when
no compound tag has been collapsed.
#+end_quote

That is the doctrine's content. The schema (~codev/schema.org~) is how a
human declares this in writing. The firewall plugin (~codev/firewall.org~)
is how the discipline gets enforced at test-time.

* The two modes

Registers tag /artefacts/. Modes tag /pipeline steps/. A pipeline step is
in one of two modes, and the obligations are different.

** Mode 1 — ~reconstruction-and-simulation~

/Definition/: the step's goal is to model a real system — to recover what
happened, to estimate what is true, or to simulate what would happen under
specified conditions. Measurements are the ground truth that the step's
outputs are accountable to.

/Examples/: a quality-control pipeline that flags suspect IMIS readings.
A gap-filling step. A climate-reanalysis product. A retrieval algorithm
that turns Sentinel-2 reflectances into a vegetation-index map. A
neuronal-tissue reconstruction. A landslide-susceptibility model.

/Obligations under this mode/:

- Every register-promotion must be declared with a ~derivation:~ entry
  (for ~measurement~ → ~derived-from-measurement~) or a ~synthesis:~ entry
  (for any flow to ~synthetic-acknowledged~).
- Test coverage is required. The manifest must reference at least one
  test that the step has passed; the checksum of the test-validated
  output must be recorded in ~last_validated_against_tests:~.
- LLM-assistance must be recorded in ~llm_assistance:~ if the step's
  code was produced or modified with LLM help. The field has a
  ~reviewer:~ requirement.

** Mode 2 — ~expression~

/Definition/: the step's goal is to render an experience, communicate an
insight, or produce an artefact whose value is in /how it conveys/ rather
than in /what it predicts/. Measurements are a starting palette, not a
ground truth the step must reproduce.

/Examples/: a poster figure for a public-engagement event. A
pedagogical animation. An artistic visualisation of model output. A
schematic in a teaching slide. A rendered scene in a digital-twin
gallery.

/Obligations under this mode/:

- All outputs must carry the ~expression-mode~ register (possibly compound
  with their input registers, but ~expression-mode~ must be present).
- All ~measurement~ or ~derived-from-measurement~ inputs must be cited.
- Downstream consumption is *firewalled*: an ~expression-mode~ output
  may not flow into a ~reconstruction-and-simulation~ step without an
  explicit ~re-measurement:~ declaration in the consuming step's
  manifest. The re-measurement declaration acknowledges that the
  pipeline is re-extracting data from an artistic rendering, and
  records the procedure.

** Why two modes and not three (or seven)

The first version of this doctrine had four modes: reconstruction,
simulation, expression, and exploration. Reconstruction and simulation
were collapsed because their obligations turned out to be identical —
both treat measurements as ground truth, both require derivation
declarations, both require test coverage. The difference between them
(reconstruction = measurements of a past or present state; simulation =
measurements as boundary conditions for a hypothetical state) is
content, not discipline.

Exploration was removed because it kept becoming a wildcard. A pipeline
step labelled "exploration" was being used as an excuse to skip
derivation declarations and test coverage. The discipline failed
silently. The two-mode polymorphism is partly a doctrinal stance:
/there is no mode in which the firewall doesn't apply./ An "exploratory"
analysis whose outputs go nowhere is fine — it just doesn't have a
manifest. The moment its outputs are committed to the repository or
shown to a colleague, it has become reconstruction (if the outputs are
meant to be true) or expression (if the outputs are meant to convey).
There is no third option.

If a use case appears to require a third mode, surface it for doctrinal
review. Almost always, the case fits one of the two with the right
compound register.

** The polymorphism

The same dataset can serve both modes — usually does, in any active
project — but a manifest must declare which mode is active at each step.
A QC-filtered IMIS temperature column might be the output of a
~reconstruction-and-simulation~ step, then become an input to an
~expression-mode~ step that produces a hero-figure for a museum
installation. Both steps have manifests. Each declares its mode. The
firewall reads the modes and applies the right obligations to each.

The polymorphism is the doctrine's name for /the same artefact carrying
different obligations depending on which step is operating on it/.

* The B/T/M failure taxonomy

When the firewall finds a problem, it classifies the problem into one of
three classes. The classification is borrowed from MāyāLucIA's review
discipline (specifically mayadev's review-pass conventions) and adapted
to manifest-failure cases.

** B-class — Blocking

A *register-tag violation*. The schema is wrong, the firewall is broken,
or the doctrine has been violated. Examples:

- An output declared ~measurement~ has inputs that are not ~measurement~
  and an operation that transforms them.
- A compound register has been collapsed to a single tag (e.g., a column
  declared ~derived-from-measurement~ has rows that were filled by a
  learned imputation model, but the ~synthetic-acknowledged~ tag is
  missing from the compound).
- An ~expression-mode~ output flows into a ~reconstruction-and-simulation~
  step with no ~re-measurement:~ declaration.
- A manifest's ~mode:~ field is absent or is not one of the two valid
  modes.

B-class failures *block the build*. They cannot be acknowledged-and-passed.
They must be fixed.

** T-class — Tighten-on-commit

An *output checksum has drifted* since the manifest's
~last_validated_against_tests:~ value. The pipeline has changed (possibly
correctly, possibly not) and the tests have not been re-run against the
new output.

T-class failures *block the build until a human decides*:

- /Real drift/: the change is intentional, the new output is the correct
  output, the tests need re-running, and the new checksum becomes the new
  ~last_validated_against_tests:~. Human commits the manifest with the
  new checksum.
- /Stale validation/: the change is unintentional, the tests have not
  been run, and the change reflects a real bug. Human reverts.

The discipline is that the decision happens /at commit time/, with the
human present, not later from a report.

** M-class — Missed (methodology)

A *required tag, field, or declaration is missing*. Examples:

- ~mode:~ field absent.
- ~register:~ tag missing on an input or output.
- ~tests:~ entry absent.
- ~last_validated_against_tests:~ checksum field absent (when ~tests:~ is
  present).
- ~llm_assistance:~ field missing when the commit author differs from
  the manifest-recorded author and the step's code has changed.

M-class failures *fail fast with a message naming exactly what is
missing*. The message is the discipline's surface — the human reads it
and knows what to write. Reports defer that. Failures force it.

** The output is the failure message

A common request when first encountering this discipline is: /can sakshi
generate a report of all the manifests in my repository, showing which
are compliant and which are not?/

No. That request is a category error. The discipline lives in the moment
of failure, when a human is at the keyboard with a decision to make.
A report defers the decision to /later/, to /someone else/, to a /review
meeting/. The discipline disappears into the bureaucracy of recording.

The pytest plugin fails the build. The message tells the human what is
wrong and what to do. The human acts. The next build passes, or it
fails again with a different message, and the human acts again. This
is the loop. It is small. It is the point.

If a report is needed for an external audit — a funding review, a
publication requirement — it can be generated *from the manifests
themselves*, by a separate tool, after the manifests have been brought
to a compliant state by the firewall's failure-and-fix loop. The
report-generator is not part of sakshi. It is downstream of sakshi.
Conflating them collapses the doctrine.

* Worked example — the silent-demotion failure

To make the doctrine concrete, here is the canonical failure case the
discipline is designed to catch. The example is small. Read it slowly.

A researcher has a CSV of hourly IMIS temperature readings from
Weissfluhjoch for January 2024. The CSV has columns ~timestamp~,
~temperature_celsius~, ~qc_flag~. The ~qc_flag~ column carries the IMIS
standard QC codes: ~0~ = passed all checks, ~1~ = flagged for review,
~9~ = missing.

The researcher wants to produce a clean daily-mean temperature time
series for a downstream analysis.

The researcher asks an LLM coding assistant: /"Give me a Python script
that loads this CSV and produces a daily-mean temperature, handling
missing values."/

The assistant produces something like:

#+begin_src python
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("imis_wfj_jan2024.csv", parse_dates=["timestamp"])
df = df.set_index("timestamp")
df["temperature_celsius"] = df["temperature_celsius"].interpolate(method="time")
daily = df["temperature_celsius"].resample("D").mean()
daily.to_csv("imis_wfj_jan2024_daily.csv")
#+end_src

The script runs. It produces output. The output looks reasonable. The
researcher commits.

What has happened, in register terms:

1. Original ~temperature_celsius~: ~measurement~ (with some ~9~-flagged
   rows that are absent in the physical world).
2. After ~.interpolate(method="time")~: silently became
   ~derived-from-measurement~ on the rows that were ~9~-flagged, and
   *still labelled as if it were the original measurement column* in the
   downstream CSV.
3. After ~.resample("D").mean()~: ~derived-from-measurement~ as a daily
   aggregate, but inheriting the silent demotion from step 2.

The downstream consumer reads ~imis_wfj_jan2024_daily.csv~ and treats it
as a daily-mean derived from hourly measurements. They produce
uncertainty estimates assuming all hours contributed real measurements.
The estimates are too tight.

What sakshi would have caught, /if/ the manifest discipline had been in
place:

- M-class: the script has no manifest. Build fails. Researcher writes a
  manifest declaring inputs (~register: measurement~), outputs
  (~register: derived-from-measurement~), and the derivation:
  ~temporal-interpolation-then-daily-mean~.
- B-class: the manifest, once written, would force the researcher to
  reckon with the ~9~-flagged rows. The interpolation step silently
  closes them. If the interpolation is across a gap longer than some
  threshold (sakshi's default: 6 hours), the closed values are no longer
  ~derived-from-measurement~ but ~synthetic-acknowledged~. The
  researcher updates the script to emit a per-row flag, and the output
  register becomes the compound ~derived-from-measurement |
  synthetic-acknowledged~.

The result is the same daily-mean CSV, but with a flag column and a
compound register tag. The downstream consumer's uncertainty estimates
are now correct. The discipline did its job.

The LLM was not malicious. The script was not wrong. The /workflow/ was
missing a step. sakshi puts the step back in.

* Worked example — the expression-firewall failure

The second canonical case. Briefer.

An ecologist produces an artistic visualisation of an alpine valley for
a public-engagement event. The visualisation is a 3D rendering. It
takes a real digital elevation model (~measurement~), drapes a Sentinel-2
RGB composite over it (~derived-from-measurement~), and exaggerates the
vertical scale by a factor of 1.5 for visual impact. The output is a
high-resolution PNG, declared in its manifest as
~register: derived-from-measurement | expression-mode~ and
~mode: expression~.

A year later, a different researcher finds the PNG on the institutional
server. They want elevation data for a watershed-area calculation. The
PNG is the only file they can find. They write a script to extract
pixel-height values from the rendering and compute slopes.

If their script has a manifest, the firewall fires:

- The PNG's manifest declares ~expression-mode~.
- The new script's manifest declares ~mode: reconstruction-and-simulation~.
- An ~expression-mode~ artefact may not flow into a
  ~reconstruction-and-simulation~ step without a ~re-measurement:~
  declaration.
- B-class failure. Build blocked.

The new researcher reads the failure. They have two paths:

1. /Add a ~re-measurement:~ declaration/ that acknowledges the
   vertical-exaggeration factor (1.5), reverses it explicitly, and notes
   that the pixel-extracted values are now ~derived-from-expression~ — a
   compound register that downstream consumers will see as carrying an
   expression-mode ancestor.
2. /Find the original DEM/ and use it directly.

Either is acceptable. Either is /honest/. The previous arrangement —
silently extracting pixel values from a stylised rendering and using
them as measurements — was the actual failure. The firewall caught it.

* Coda — what this doctrine assumes about the practitioner

sakshi's doctrine assumes its practitioner is a /careful person under
time pressure/. It does not assume they are infallible. It does not
assume they have memorised the doctrine. It assumes they will write a
manifest /because the build forces them to/, and that the manifest's
required fields will prompt the right reckoning at the moment of
writing.

The doctrine is not a moral framework. It is a tool. The four registers
and two modes are chosen because they are the minimum vocabulary
sufficient to catch the failure cases that matter in LLM-assisted
environmental-data work. They are not the final word. A different
domain — clinical trials, financial reporting, criminal forensics — would
need different registers and different modes. The /structure/ of the
discipline (named registers, named modes, fail-fast on violation) is
portable. The /content/ is domain-specific.

sakshi v0.1 ships the structure with environmental-data content. Other
domains can fork the doctrine and replace the content. The schema
(~codev/schema.org~) is the seam where the content lives. The firewall
(~codev/firewall.org~) is the seam where the enforcement lives. Both
are deliberately small, so that forking is cheap.

— /End of doctrine./
